# VC fund evaluation framework

Three core questions, fourteen sub-criteria. Use these as the keys for the structured output.

## 1. Can we trust this team?

- **`response_to_failure`** — Does the team describe specific mistakes from earlier funds, not vague "the market shifted" explanations? Look for concreteness: e.g. *"We overestimated the tech angle in a sector where the business model was fundamentally traditional."*
- **`process_improvement`** — Evidence they changed processes to prevent recurrence of those mistakes. A described mistake without a corresponding process change is half-credit.
- **`consistency`** — Do statements from different team members across roles and seniority cohere? Usually only assessable from interview content. From documents alone, mark `"not present"`.
- **`founder_relationships`** — How do they behave toward struggling founders? Engagement, helpfulness, follow-through. Usually requires founder reference calls. From documents alone, mark `"not present"`.
- **`gp_alignment`** — Real skin in the game. Look for explicit dollar amount or percentage of fund size, transparent commitment structure. Boilerplate ("GPs are aligned with LPs") without numbers is low confidence.

## 2. How does their strategy work?

- **`plausibility`** — Does the investment thesis logically align with the fund's time and resource constraints? Counter-example to flag: a solo GP claiming months of conviction-building per company across hundreds of investments. Watch for math that doesn't fit a 24-hour day.
- **`end_to_end_process`** — Clear picture of deal sourcing through exit. Sourcing channels, conviction process, portfolio support model, exit strategy. Gaps in any stage are gaps.
- **`portfolio_management`** — Portfolio construction, cash flow, LP expectations, deal velocity. Look for explicit reserves policy, follow-on strategy, deployment pace.
- **`skill_signal`** — Track-record link between supposed edge and outcomes. The metric stress-test from Step 4 attaches here. *Did the strategy actually produce the result, or could the result have come from something else?*
- **`metrics_interpretation`** — How do they interpret their own metrics? Look for nuance — e.g. acknowledging that a low loss ratio could reflect follow-on strategy rather than picking skill. Self-aware metric interpretation is itself a positive signal.

## 3. What are the risks, and can we live with them?

- **`key_person_concentration`** — Who steps in if a key partner leaves? Stabilization timeline? Look for an actual succession plan, not boilerplate.
- **`market_or_team_novelty`** — Is the strategy unproven, or is the team executing an unfamiliar strategy? Both are risks; the deck should acknowledge them.
- **`conflict_of_interest`** — Policies (or lack thereof) for known conflicts. Look for explicit disclosure and concrete mitigation, not "we will manage."
- **`risk_acceptance`** — Willingness to live with mitigated vs. unmitigated risks. Often only assessable in conversation. From documents alone, usually mark `"not present"`.

## Notes on coverage

Several sub-criteria (`consistency`, `founder_relationships`, `risk_acceptance`) are inherently conversational and should usually be marked `"not present"` when only document inputs are available. This is correct behaviour, not a limitation. These belong in the gap list as call topics, not in the memo as document-derived findings.

Where a sub-criterion is partially supported by documents but inherently call-dependent for full coverage (e.g. `gp_alignment` may show numbers but the alignment posture comes through in conversation), tag with the documents' confidence level and route the conversational follow-up to `what_would_change_the_call`.
